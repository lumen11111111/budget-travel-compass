# BTC-001 Research Notes

## Commission

- Production title: **How to Verify Accessible Accommodation for a Family Trip**
- Primary query: `how to find accessible family accommodation`
- Tier / freshness: Tier A / HIGH
- Reader outcome: turn one traveler's functional requirements into property-specific questions, evidence, written confirmation, and a fallback.
- Does not cover: a full multigenerational itinerary, legal-compliance advice, or a universal definition of “accessible.”

## Claim plan

1. Explain why an accessibility label or filter is only a discovery signal.
2. Separate the journey into arrival, entrance, room, bathroom, shared-space, and emergency requirements.
3. Use dimensions, photos, routes, equipment, and operating details rather than broad labels.
4. Treat U.S. ADA rules and platform definitions as jurisdiction- or platform-specific examples, not global standards.
5. Require written property confirmation for trip-critical features and a failure plan.

## Source findings

- U.S. ADA Title III rules require covered lodging reservation services to describe accessible features in enough detail for an individual to assess fit and to block a reserved accessible room. This is a U.S.-specific legal example.
- UK government guidance tells disabled travelers to state requirements specifically, contact accommodation directly, seek written confirmation, and verify room, bathroom, lift, and step-free details.
- Airbnb currently offers feature-level accessibility filters and requires hosts to provide photographs for listed features. Its definitions include measurable criteria, but apply only to Airbnb's system.
- Airbnb itself recommends selecting priority filters and reviewing the individual listing, which supports the distinction between filtering and verification.

## Outline validation

The draft uses a “journey through the property” structure rather than a generic checklist: needs profile, route, room/bathroom, shared areas, written confirmation, then fallback. The question sheet is the practical output.
