# How to Plan a Multigenerational Family Trip

Put the actual travelers around one planning brief before choosing the destination. Ask each person about pace, mobility, budget comfort, privacy, support and the experience they most want; a family role cannot answer those questions for them.

The plan needs a small shared core and credible ways to branch. That creates enough time together to give the trip meaning while protecting individual choice.

## Collect individual trip conditions

Privately record each person's non-negotiables, access needs, preferred energy level, budget comfort, privacy, food or schedule constraints, and one desired experience. Do not translate “grandparent,” “child,” or “parent” into an ability profile or decision rank.

## Choose a small shared core

Pick one or two shared anchors per day: a meal, event, short outing or time at the base. Protect arrival and departure from ambitious group plans. The core should be valuable even if optional branches change.

## Build branches without making them second class

Offer alternatives that differ by interest, effort or duration—not “real activity” versus “easy option.” A museum and a garden, or a long walk and a neighborhood lunch, can both be complete choices. State when and where the group reunites.

## Make support and care explicit

Ask what help is wanted, who agrees to provide it, and when that person is off duty. Support may involve navigation, language, luggage, medication reminders already requested, childcare or mobility assistance. Do not assign it automatically to one gender, generation or family role.

Separate decision rights: who chooses shared spending, who can opt out, and which changes require everyone. Align the group budget before booking; individual comfort with spending may differ even within one household.

## Rehearse one mixed-age scenario

For a grandparent–grandchild day, begin with their actual interests and agreements. Perhaps both choose the shared morning while one rests and the other joins a separate afternoon activity with another adult. This is one scenario, not the model for every family.
