# BTC-008 Research Notes

## Commission

- Production title: **How to Choose a Local Boutique Hotel Without Paying for Style Alone**
- Query: `how to choose a boutique hotel`
- Tier / freshness: Tier A / HIGH
- Outcome: distinguish visual appeal from functional value using a property-evidence scorecard.
- Does not cover: choosing among hotel, hostel, and rental categories.

## Claim plan

1. Ask the reader to define which small-property qualities would improve this trip.
2. Audit gallery evidence against room function, building movement, location, staffing, noise, and amenities.
3. Calculate the total stay price and compare it with a deliberately selected alternative.
4. Treat every “boutique” promise as property-specific marketing until supported by direct evidence.

## Source findings

- The FTC's U.S. lodging fee rule supports comparing mandatory total price rather than an incomplete advertised rate.
- Expedia Group's current lodging API requirements illustrate the need to separate total, taxes/fees, pay-now, pay-later, and deposit information.
- Google Maps supports future departure/arrival time planning for driving and transit; this is a route-estimation tool, not a guarantee of service.
- U.S. ADA reservation rules illustrate why feature-level detail is more useful than an accessibility label, but apply only in that jurisdiction.

## Structural decision

The article is an evidence audit: promise, proof, friction, alternative, verdict. The scorecard has evidence-strength and trip-value columns, unlike BTC-007's type matrix.
