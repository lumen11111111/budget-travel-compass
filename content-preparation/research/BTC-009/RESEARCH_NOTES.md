# BTC-009 Research Notes

## Commission

- Production title: **How to Choose a Stay for Remote Work**
- Query: `how to choose accommodation for remote work`
- Tier / freshness: Tier A / HIGH
- Outcome: verify whether a stay supports the reader's actual calls, focused work, power, privacy, time zone, and recovery—with backups for critical failures.
- Does not cover: visas, tax residency, a full digital-nomad routine, or a tech packing list.

## Claim plan

1. Convert the work calendar into testable accommodation requirements.
2. Treat a platform Wi-Fi badge as limited evidence, not a reliability guarantee.
3. Verify desk/chair, outlets, noise, privacy, climate, and backup logistics through direct evidence.
4. Use application-owned bandwidth guidance only for the reader's actual tools.
5. Produce a pass/fail work-readiness checklist with failure tolerances.

## Source findings

- Airbnb lets hosts test and publish Wi-Fi speed; its documentation says the test is taken at the property and can display a “fast Wi-Fi” highlight at 50 Mbps or above.
- Airbnb's own material describes the dedicated-workspace amenity as a table/desk, outlet, and comfortable chair, which is not an ergonomic or room-privacy guarantee.
- Microsoft Teams documents different minimum, recommended, and best-performance bandwidth by call mode and explains that real use varies with layout and resolution.
- Therefore a download number alone cannot establish call quality throughout a property or at the reader's working hours; the draft treats that conclusion as editorial synthesis.

## Structural decision

The article works backward from the reader's highest-consequence work event, then builds proof and fallback layers. Its utility is a gated checklist, not the weighted comparison used in BTC-007.
