# BTC-010 Research Notes

## Commission

- Production title: **How to Check Whether a Neighborhood Is Right for Your Stay**
- Query: `how to choose a safe neighborhood to stay in`
- Tier / freshness: Tier A / HIGH
- Outcome: evaluate the actual routes and hours a traveler will use, combine official and recent evidence, record uncertainty, and keep a workable fallback.
- Does not cover: declaring any neighborhood universally safe/unsafe or a complete arrival protocol.

## Claim plan

1. Replace citywide reputation with trip-specific routes and times.
2. Use official travel advice within its nationality and geographic scope.
3. Explain the limits of crime rankings and review anecdotes.
4. Verify transit with the operator after using map tools for route discovery.
5. Include accessibility, lighting, activity, late return, and total friction without implying control over other people's harmful behavior.

## Source findings

- U.S. State Department advisories explicitly apply to U.S. citizens/nationals/legal residents and may identify location-specific risks inside a destination.
- UK FCDO advice is guidance for British travelers and includes safety, health, entry, and legal differences; nationality scope matters.
- FBI UCR guidance warns that rankings and direct comparisons can create misleading perceptions and that reported data exclude unreported crime.
- Google Maps can show planned-time routes and some scheduled or real-time transit information, while acknowledging that data availability depends on operators.

## Structural decision

The article follows four “evidence lenses”—route, official context, recent signals, and friction—before a worksheet. It avoids a safety checklist and does not rank locations.
