# BTC-011 Research Notes

## Commission

- Production title: **Basic Economy and Baggage Fees: Compare the Real Flight Cost**
- Query: `basic economy baggage fees total cost`
- Tier / freshness: Tier A / HIGH
- Outcome: normalize two or more flight offers after traveler-required bags, seats, flexibility, booking channel, and connection costs are priced.
- Does not cover: a packing list, flexible-date searching, or naming one airline cheapest.

## Claim plan

1. Define traveler requirements before opening fare-family comparisons.
2. Require route-, date-, traveler-, channel-, and status-specific rules.
3. Separate mandatory advertised fare from optional-but-required-for-this-traveler costs.
4. Preserve policy evidence and identify the ticket seller/servicer.
5. Avoid stale reliance on the vacated 2024 U.S. ancillary-fee disclosure rule.

## Source findings

- Current U.S. DOT ticket-buying guidance says advertised airfare includes mandatory taxes/fees, while optional services and restrictions still require comparison. It directs travelers to airline baggage-fee pages.
- DOT's July 2026 update says the 2024 ancillary-fee mandates were vacated and the restored framework requires notice that baggage fees may apply plus a central airline fee location. The draft does not claim passenger-specific upfront fee disclosure is currently required.
- Delta's current “Main Basic” page documents limited flexibility and route/origin-specific exceptions; it also says to check the ticket and fee page.
- American's current 2026 Basic Economy documentation shows seat and bag rules can change by ticket date and product; it is an example, not a cross-airline rule.
- Google Flights says its bag-fee filter uses partner data and advises checking airline size, weight, and price rules.

## Structural decision

The article is a calculator workshop: requirement card, fare evidence card, component calculation, channel check, stress test, fictional demonstration. It is not a generic checklist.
