# BTC-013 Research Notes

## Commission

- Production title: **How to Find Cheaper Flights with Flexible Dates**
- Primary query: `how to find cheap flights with flexible dates`
- Tier / freshness: Tier A / HIGH
- Reader outcome: a repeatable search sequence that compares equivalent itineraries and records a usable total, not merely the lowest calendar number.
- Does not cover: alternate-airport ground-cost analysis (BTC-014), shoulder-season selection (BTC-015), or a full basic-economy/baggage explainer (BTC-011).

## Claim plan

1. Explain what flexible-date tools actually do: expose prices for multiple date combinations and trip lengths; they do not guarantee future availability or the future lowest price.
2. Separate the discovery price from the usable total after required bags, seats, booking-channel restrictions, self-transfers, schedule quality, and currency/payment effects.
3. Explain that fare families can carry materially different restrictions and inclusions.
4. Require airline-direct verification immediately before payment.
5. Avoid fixed-day, fixed-lead-time, platform-superiority, and price-prediction claims.

## Source findings

- Google Flights documents Dates and Price Graph tools for exploring fares across days, weeks, or months. Its “Best” and “Cheapest” views optimize for different trade-offs; cheaper results can include self-transfers, airport changes, or OTA options.
- Google states that flight prices change and prediction-based guidance can be wrong. Its search inventory is partner-dependent, so it is not a complete market guarantee.
- Google’s bag-fee filter uses information received from partners; estimates can differ from the airline’s current size, weight, route, fare, tax, or status rules.
- The U.S. DOT explains that advertised airfare includes mandatory taxes and fees, while optional services and fare restrictions still require comparison. The rule applies to covered sales; the article does not universalize U.S. regulation.
- American Airlines’ current fare comparison demonstrates why “economy” is not a single product: its Basic/Main products differ in changes, seat selection, bags, and other attributes. It is used as an example, not a universal airline rule.

## Outline validation

The approved outline is retained: define useful flexibility; set windows and trip length; scan calendars; shortlist combinations; normalize fare conditions; verify with the airline; record a buy decision. The draft adds a working Search Log without expanding into the three sibling topics.
