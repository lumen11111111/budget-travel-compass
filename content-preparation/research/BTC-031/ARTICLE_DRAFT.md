# How to Split Group Trip Costs Fairly

An equal split answers an arithmetic question. A fair split answers a group question: who used the expense, who agreed to it, and which rule did everyone accept before money moved? The simplest system is the one the group can explain in one sentence and apply without renegotiating every receipt.

## Agree on the cost categories first

Use three starting categories. **Shared** costs serve the whole confirmed group and follow the agreed group rule. **Individual** costs belong to one traveler. **Usage-based** costs are divided among the people who used or opted into them.

Accommodation may be shared equally, divided by room or bed arrangement, or adjusted for a materially different room—whatever the group agrees before booking. BTC-006 handles the accommodation configuration itself. Activities, groceries and local transport should enter the category that matches actual participation, not habit.

## Write the rule before the purchase

For each recurring type, record the category, participants, and calculation. Decide how to handle deposits, optional upgrades, unused portions, and small shared groceries. Avoid creating a rule so precise that nobody will maintain it.

**Fictional demonstration:** Four travelers share a 240-unit expense equally: 240 ÷ 4 = 60 each. A separate 90-unit activity used by three travelers is 90 ÷ 3 = 30 for those three. These figures demonstrate arithmetic only; they are not prices or recommendations.

## Give the payer evidence, not permanent responsibility

One person paying first does not make them the group's bank. Enter the expense when it occurs: description, payer, participants, split rule, original currency, amount, and receipt or booking evidence. The ledger calculates what is owed; it should not rely on chat memory.

If currencies differ, choose one conversion method and evidence point for the group, record both original and settlement currency, and keep any provider fee visible. This article does not prescribe an exchange service or accounting treatment.

## Handle cancellations through two separate questions

First, what does the provider's exact policy return, to whom, and in which currency? Second, how does the group's pre-agreed rule allocate any unrecovered amount? A voluntary withdrawal, provider cancellation, and group decision to change plans need not use the same rule.

Do not promise a refund from an app or booking platform without current transaction evidence. Keep the provider result and the group allocation as separate ledger lines.

## Settle in stages

Collect large commitments near booking so one payer is not exposed for months. Run a mid-trip settlement when balances become material, then a final close after refunds and pending charges resolve. Pick a minimum amount below which the group prefers to net balances at the end.
