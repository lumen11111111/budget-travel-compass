# BTC-035 Research Notes

## Commission

- Production title: **How to Make a Realistic Budget for a Weekend Trip**
- Primary query: `weekend trip budget`
- Tier / freshness: Tier B / MEDIUM
- Reader outcome: a complete three-scenario worksheet that turns current quotes and personal allowances into a go/no-go ceiling.
- Does not cover: saving habits (BTC-034), group settlement (BTC-031), or destination price lists.

## Claim plan

1. Treat a budget as a written spending plan based on explicit categories and actual/quoted amounts.
2. Separate fixed committed costs, per-person costs, per-day costs, conditional costs, and contingency.
3. Include transport, accommodation, food, local movement, activities, booking fees, taxes, baggage, relevant connectivity, and contingency.
4. Build BASE, COMFORTABLE, and MAXIMUM from the same trip facts rather than three arbitrary price points.
5. Use formulas and a fictional worked example dated 2026-08-14; do not imply example prices are typical.

## Source findings

- Consumer.gov defines a budget as a written plan and recommends listing expenses, comparing them with available income, recording actual spending, and adjusting the next plan.
- CFPB guidance supports realistic category-based budgeting, tracking, and including irregular/miscellaneous costs rather than editing a budget to an aspirational number.
- U.S. DOT ticket guidance distinguishes mandatory airfare components from optional services, supporting separate baggage/seat/change rows when relevant. The article frames this as a comparison principle, not a global disclosure rule.

## Outline validation

The article follows the approved order: trip facts and usable hours; fixed transport/stay; food/movement/activities; fees/taxes/bags/connectivity/contingency; three scenarios; tracking and decision rules. The worksheet is the center of the article.
