# BTC-042 Research Notes

## Commission

- Production title: **How to Plan Your First Solo Female Trip**
- Primary query: `first solo female trip planning`
- Tier / freshness: Tier A / HIGH
- Reader outcome: choose a manageable first-trip shape and verify destination, lodging, transport, communication, boundaries, and backups without ranking places as “safe.”
- Does not cover: a complete arrival protocol (BTC-041), neighborhood research guide (BTC-010), hostel-versus-tour comparison (BTC-043), or a solo-weekend itinerary (BTC-045).

## Claim plan

1. Safety is destination-, route-, time-, identity-, and circumstance-specific; no destination is guaranteed safe.
2. Use the traveler’s own government advisory, destination/local authority, official transport operator, property documentation, and recent carefully interpreted experience signals.
3. Make the first trip operationally manageable: simple route, reasonable arrival, verified lodging, controllable transport, communication and money backups.
4. Present boundaries and exit decisions without blaming a traveler for another person’s actions.
5. Make emergency and consular details destination- and nationality-specific.

## Source findings

- U.S., Canadian, Australian, and UK government guidance consistently directs women/solo travelers to destination-specific official advice, local laws/customs, reputable transport, accommodation planning, trusted-contact itineraries, emergency contacts, and backup access to information or money.
- The sources do not justify a universal “safe country” list. Their value is the verification workflow and the reminder that risks and official response differ by destination and traveler.
- Some official pages contain prescriptive or fear-heavy language. The draft extracts operational checks, avoids universal clothing/behavior rules, and states that preparation cannot guarantee safety or transfer responsibility for harm.

## Outline validation

The article follows the approved commissioning sequence: manageable trip shape; evidence-based destination research; lodging and arrival timing; communication and documents; boundaries and transport; readiness check. Arrival, neighborhoods, format comparison, and itinerary mechanics remain summarized and deferred to sibling briefs.
